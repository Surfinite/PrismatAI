> Date: 2026-06-13. Method: 7 parallel dimension auditors, every finding adversarially
> re-verified by an independent agent (code re-read + read-only probes on the live Jun-13 build).
> Mandate: question *why each piece exists*, judge against good RL practice, hunt silent skews —
> NOT docs-conformance. Prior audits (06-09/10/11/12) were treated as claims, not truths.
> Scope: read-only. No engine rebuild, no full multi-hour iteration (probes are single-state /
> smoke / committed-artifact based; flagged where a claim rests on extrapolation).
>
> Companion living docs: `eval/rl_campaign.md`, `eval/rl_runbook.md`, `eval/campaign_log.md`,
> `eval/README.md`. This doc is a point-in-time audit record; disposition items into the
> `campaign_log.md` limitations register.

## TL;DR — the load-bearing conclusion

**The campaign, as framed and frozen, cannot answer its own question, and the thing it was built
to fix is already fixed without RL.** Two independent, verified results combine into this:

1. **The premise has collapsed (CRITICAL).** The campaign exists to stop the deployed AI
   *over-clicking* Infusion Grid (Hotel). But the over-click was an **action-space artifact of the
   NARROW auto-fire iterator**, not a value-net flaw — and the **config-only action-space widening
   (the IGsubset root iterator) already closes it with the untrained v221 net** (proven: ktink_t9 at
   c=0.3/N=1000 fires **2** IGs on the narrow root, **1** on the IGsubset root; that widening already
   ships in production via `AITools.cpp:277`). Under the frozen IGsubset config v221 never over-clicks
   across 18 IG-live probe states (argmax IG-count ∈ {0,1}, never ≥2). The campaign measures
   candidate-vs-parent **both under the widened space**, so it is **structurally blind to the only
   intervention that removed the bug**, and there is no over-click gap left for RL to close. If
   anything the residual gap is *under*-clicking (ktink), which value-only RL on labels generated by
   the under-clicking net is unlikely to fix.

2. **Even if a gap existed, the experiment can't resolve it (HIGH × several).** IG is a live decision
   in <5% of records (max feasible count 2), yielding single-digit on-axis counterfactuals/iteration
   against a per-iteration eval that only fires REJECT at ≈−10pp and a checkpoint that resolves ±3.5pp.
   A whole-game-outcome value target carries almost no credit signal for one per-turn micro-decision.
   A flat `d_rl` is the prior expectation under *both* "RL works" and "RL doesn't" — the design cannot
   discriminate.

**Recommendation:** do **not** run the IG campaign to "see what happens." Either (a) re-measure
whether v221 has *any* exploitable IG gap under the exact frozen config with a powered behavioural
metric before committing iterations (current evidence says no over-click gap; a possible under-click
gap), or (b) pick a proof-of-life axis with a **demonstrated parent gap under the deployed search**,
or (c) reframe the goal and re-baseline. Separately, fix the design-soundness items below that would
bite *any* RL axis on this loop (off-policy buffer, promote-unless-harm drift, value-target SNR,
between-checkpoint strength blindness).

The loop is, to its credit, **mechanically excellent**: sound value-orientation seam (one negation,
verified), correct label/perspective machinery (18 tests pass), correct Wilson math, genuine active
provenance on the eval path, a disciplined 15-check preflight, and internally-consistent IG
instrumentation. The problems are **design soundness and premise**, not (mostly) bugs.

A candidate **sixth feature skew** (fragile-unit `is_blocking`) was reported and then **RETRACTED on
review (2026-06-14)** — it was a false positive from a malformed test fixture. The live/AS3 freeze rule
is `disruptDamage >= current HP`, which the C++ `isFrozen()` implements correctly; the JS corpus and the
C++ recompute agree on real turn-start data. See F-SKEW-1 (RETRACTED) for the full reconciliation. Net:
**this audit did NOT find a real new skew** — the feature surface is clean (three-way gate green;
209 self-play sidecars / 11,264 instances exporter==inference, 0 mismatches).

## Severity-ordered findings

Severities are the **post-verification** ratings. "V:" = verifier verdict. Each finding answers
*why the reason for the thing is unsound*, not merely "differs from docs".

### CRITICAL

#### C1 — The campaign's foundational premise (AI over-clicks IG) is already false under the frozen config
- **Dimension:** axis_premise · **V: CONFIRMED (critical, high confidence)**
- **What:** Under `HardIterator_5var_IGsubset_Root` + c=0.3 + v221, the parent net does not over-click:
  18 IG-live states → argmax IG-count ∈ {0,1}, never ≥2; tactical suite 4 PASS / 1 FAIL where the
  one FAIL is an *under*-click (ktink_t9 wants 1, fires 0).
- **Why unsound:** The over-click was an artifact of the **narrow auto-fire action space** (verified:
  narrow root fires 2, IGsubset root fires 1 on the same state, same c, same N), removed by the
  config-only IGsubset widening **already in production** (`AITools.cpp:277`). A single-axis RL
  campaign whose goal is "learn to stop over-clicking" has no gap to close once a non-RL change closed
  it. The answer-producing eval (candidate vs parent) runs **both legs on the widened space**
  (`rl_campaign.md:154-158` deliberately excludes the narrow anchor), so it cannot credit the actual
  fix. A flat result is pre-ordained, not an RL verdict.
- **Evidence:** `query_move.js ktink_t9` @ narrow/c0.3/N1000 → IG=2; @ IGsubset/c0.3/N1000 → IG=1.
  `tactical_baseline.json` (committed) + live `tactical_suite.py` run. README at
  `eval/tactical_cases/README.md:112-135` itself states the over-click was at the OLD c=2.0/narrow path
  and "the net underrates the 1-IG defensive line" at c=0.3.
- **Fix:** Before any iterations, measure v221's IG behaviour **under the exact frozen config** with a
  powered behavioural metric (paired IG-click-rate on an IG-live panel). If no over-click gap (current
  evidence), re-pose the axis (close the *under*-click gap, or pick an axis with a demonstrated gap)
  and re-baseline. Do **not** let a flat IG result trigger the A9 "value-only RL is the limit" stop.

### HIGH

#### H1 — IG signal is below the noise floor: <5% of records, single-digit counterfactuals/iter
- **Dimensions:** axis_premise + exploration + labels_training (three independent finders) · **V: CONFIRMED (high)**
- **What:** Committed `n1000_rescreen_k12.json`: 55/1121 records (4.9%) have `ig_feasible_max>0`;
  max feasible count = 2; 26 records with any IG click. ~1.7 IG-feasible decisions/forced game.
  EpsilonIG yields ~0.4 on-axis counterfactuals/forced game (docs' own estimate) → single-digit
  explored IG counterfactuals/iteration.
- **Why unsound:** ~470 optimizer steps/iter fine-tuning a net built with orders of magnitude more
  optimization, with the IG-relevant gradient diluted ~100:1, against an eval that resolves ±3.5pp
  (checkpoint) / ±8.7pp (per-iteration) and a REJECT verdict that only fires at ≈−10pp. A few-pp IG
  effect on <5% of decisions is inside the noise under any hypothesis. The experiment cannot
  discriminate "RL works" from "RL doesn't" on its named axis.
- **Fix:** Treat flat `d_rl` as uninformative by design (don't gate on it). If IG is retained,
  redesign the curriculum to maximise IG-**live** roots (force Hotel **and** its red/tech enablers so
  IG is fireable many times/game), and make the primary readout a **paired IG-click-rate delta on a
  fixed IG-live probe panel**, not aggregate win-rate.

#### H2 — `promote-unless-harm` + a low-power REJECT screen permits compounding strength drift
- **Dimension:** eval_stats · **V: CONFIRMED (high)**
- **What:** Every candidate is promoted to be the next generator unless REJECT (general Wilson
  ci_upper<0.5). Re-derived OCs at n=384: P(REJECT|parity)=2.3%, P(REJECT|−3pp)=20.7%,
  P(REJECT|−5pp)=48.8%. So a truly −3pp candidate promotes ~79% of the time; five consecutive −3pp
  candidates all pass w.p. 0.31 → a lineage ~−15pp below origin before the next checkpoint can fire.
- **Why unsound:** The promoted candidate becomes both the next generator **and** the next REJECT
  baseline, so errors compound — the classic accept-all self-play random walk. The campaign chose
  promote-unless-harm precisely so the generator *would* move, but never reconciled that with a harm
  screen whose sensitivity is ~2%. Good practice at this power is a **non-inferiority gate** (require
  ci_lower above 0.5−margin, sized to that margin) or **decoupling generator from lineage head** (keep
  generating with a fixed strong net; promote only on a powered improvement).
- **Fix:** Run the powered origin check **every iteration for the first few** (bound drift), or convert
  promotion to powered non-inferiority, or fix the generator. Bound worst-case between-checkpoint drift
  in the contract.

#### H3 — The only between-checkpoint guard (val-accuracy) is blind to game-strength regression
- **Dimension:** eval_stats · **V: CONFIRMED (high; recategorised design-soundness)**
- **What:** Between powered checkpoints (every 3–5 iters) the lineage is watched only by the
  per-iteration val-acc tripwire (abort if >3pp below parent) and the checkpoint B8 val-acc band
  (warn if >5pp below origin). No per-iteration **game-strength** measurement vs origin/lineage.
- **Why unsound:** The campaign's own docs say the supervised val is a weak proxy ("MB-flavoured val
  can't see the production-vector features; the payoff is RL, not a supervised win"). A net can drift
  several pp weaker in self-play strength while human-val moves <1pp, so a 3/5pp val band cannot bound
  game-strength drift — the H2 hazard is effectively unmonitored for up to 5 iterations. The guard
  measures the wrong quantity by design.
- **Fix:** Add a cheap per-iteration game-strength probe (small fixed-N lineage-head vs origin), or
  shorten the checkpoint cadence to 1–2 iters early. Don't guard an RL objective with a supervised proxy.

#### H4 — W=5 replay buffer blends 5 heterogeneous generators with no off-policy correction
- **Dimension:** labels_training · **V: CONFIRMED (high)**
- **What:** Stage 3 trains on the union of the last 5 self-play H5s, each weighted equally per-record
  (`rl_data.py:57-67`: weight = colour-balance × (1−frac)/sp_total, no recency/importance factor).
  Under promote-unless-harm the generator changes most iterations, so the window blends up to 5 distinct
  policies (and, via base+K seeds, 5 distinct card-set draws). `train.py:1256` even prints the differing
  per-file generator lineage.
- **Why unsound:** AlphaZero-style value loops assume a slowly-moving generator and a near-on-policy
  buffer. Here the design deliberately moves the generator *and* deliberately retains 4 older
  generations weighted equally with the newest — a moving target with zero off-policy correction
  (no recency decay, no importance weighting, no generator-tag conditioning). The promotion rationale
  is documented; the staleness it amplifies is never reconciled. (Refinement: cross-file mass is
  proportional to record count, not 1/W, so verbose generations dominate even more.)
- **Fix:** Add recency decay to the per-source weight, or shrink W toward 1–2, or run a one-off W=1 vs
  W=5 ablation on real prediction-movement + origin eval. At minimum weight the newest iteration above
  the oldest. Document the off-policy assumption.

#### H5 — Whole-game-outcome value target gives near-zero credit signal for the IG micro-decision
- **Dimension:** labels_training · **V: CONFIRMED (high)**
- **What:** Every captured record carries the single game-level `outcome_p0`. IG appears in ~5% of
  records (measured 252/4720) and is *actionable* in even fewer. The value net's only IG-specific
  signal is outcome differences between near-identical fire/skip lines (which EpsilonIG manufactures
  at ~0.4/forced game).
- **Why unsound:** A whole-game value regression is a fundamental instrument/question mismatch for
  teaching a per-turn micro-decision with a **value-only** net (no per-decision credit). The gradient
  that could teach "IG-fire was good here" is diluted across the whole trajectory and dominated by
  economy/board features. This is independent of any bug.
- **Fix:** The durable fix is the §14 policy/advantage head + PUCT (per-decision credit) or a
  contrastive objective on matched fire/skip state pairs. Until then, be explicit that flat is expected
  and don't read it as an RL verdict.

### MEDIUM

#### M1 — Interior search nodes auto-fire IG, so the search can't value a multi-turn "decline IG" line
- **Dimensions:** exploration (rated high) + axis_premise (verifier downgraded to medium) ·
  **V: NEEDS-NUANCE (medium)**
- **What:** RL_SelfPlay/RL_Eval use the IGsubset iterator at the **root only**
  (`UCTNode.cpp:37-44`); interior nodes use `HardIterator_5var`, whose ability filter
  `Ability_Filter_Live` lacks Hotel, and Hotel has `healthUsed=0` so the self-sac guard is bypassed →
  interior nodes auto-fire all ready IG every turn.
- **Why unsound (corrected):** When the search evaluates the root child "fire 0 IG now", every
  continuation in its subtree auto-fires IG on later turns — the search **cannot represent a consistent
  "decline IG" strategy**, so the value estimate that *selects among IG-count root children* is
  computed under "decline now, fire later". **Nuance from verification:** the training **labels are game
  outcomes**, not MCTS backed-up values (`SelfPlayV2Exporter.cpp:48-51`), and the realized played line
  uses the root iterator every turn — so this is **not label contamination** (as the exploration
  auditor framed it) but a **move-selection / search-quality limitation on the exact axis under study**.
  It still degrades which IG-count gets recorded and makes "skip IG" undervalued.
- **Fix:** Wire a NoIG interior iterator (building blocks `V5_CS_NoIG` exist; no assembled interior
  twin yet) so the search can represent un-fired-IG continuations, OR explicitly bound every claim to
  one-ply IG timing and re-classify in the limitations register from "accepted" to "scopes the axis".
  Note the NoIG fix biases the other way (assume IG never fires later) — neither default is neutral,
  which is itself an argument that value-only search is the wrong tool for this axis.

#### M2 — Preflight validates the RL_SelfPlay tuple but never asserts the self-play blocks USE that player
- **Dimension:** orchestration · **V: CONFIRMED (medium)**
- **What:** `check_frozen_tuple` pins RL_SelfPlay's knobs and the two blocks' rounds/ForcedCards/
  Threads/Seed/run, but never checks the blocks' `players[]` reference RL_SelfPlay (the docstring
  deliberately excludes Benchmarks player-name resolution).
- **Why unsound:** Preflight exists to catch silent campaign-identity drift before a multi-hour run.
  Freezing RL_SelfPlay's knobs is meaningless unless the blocks instantiate RL_SelfPlay. Seven
  near-identical sibling players exist (`RL_SelfPlay_N100/N256/...`, some carrying the **retired**
  EpsilonLate=0.05); a drift to a *known sibling* is caught by neither check 2 (run:true) nor the
  engine's unknown-name hard-fail, and would generate the entire corpus with the wrong exploration.
- **Fix:** Assert each `selfplay_mix` block's `players` == the expected RL_SelfPlay/group pairing.
  Cheap; closes the one drift class the frozen-tuple check leaves open. (Verifier add M-B: also assert
  `SelfPlaySampling:true` and the IGsubset `RootMoveIterator` on RL_SelfPlay — same class.)

#### M3 — Self-play H5 lineage stamp records the driver's ASSUMED generator, not the engine-confirmed one
- **Dimension:** orchestration · **V: CONFIRMED (medium)**
- **What:** Stage 2 stamps `--stamp-parent = frozen parent_bin path`; `vectorize_v2` hashes that file.
  Nothing confirms the engine actually loaded that net during self-play (`run_iteration` only greps
  the log for `WARNING:`). The eval path, by contrast, hard-fails on `engine_confirmed_load`
  (`run_eval.py:599-614`).
- **Why unsound:** Generation provenance is more load-bearing than eval provenance (it determines what
  the net trains on for W iterations), yet it is the weaker of the two. Combined with M2, a config drift
  that made self-play use a different net would be invisible: the H5 is still stamped parent_bin,
  train.py's lineage check passes, corruption persists W−1 iterations — the exact "crippled-window"
  class. (Backstop: preflight pins RL_SelfPlay.WeightsFile==parent_bin + sha; the engine FATALs on an
  unloaded net — so the *static* parent is verified, only the *runtime* load is unconfirmed.)
- **Fix:** Parse the self-play engine stderr for the RL_SelfPlay NeuralNet load line and assert it
  equals parent_bin before stamping, mirroring the eval path.

#### M4 — `a6` (the only sign-flip detector) and the three-way feature gate are MANUAL, and the engine exe is not sha-pinned
- **Dimension:** orientation · **V: CONFIRMED (medium)**
- **What:** `a6_orientation_check.py` is the sole end-to-end test of the maxPlayer-negation seam; it is
  invoked by no automated stage (only docs/triage prose). `test_three_way_feature_parity.py` is
  likewise manual. `campaign_frozen.json` pins the *weights* sha but **not** the engine exe sha.
- **Why unsound:** The two scariest, silently-green value failures (a sign inversion; a shared-C++
  feature skew like the five+one prior ones) are caught only by manual tests, and an engine rebuild —
  out-of-band, in a separate repo, with "never infer staleness from mtime" already a known hazard —
  could introduce a flip that sails through every automated gate. The mitigation (per-iteration loop
  changes only PyTorch weights, which can't flip the C++ sign) is real but makes the omission
  *defensible, not sound*: nothing enforces that a6 ran after the last rebuild.
- **Fix:** sha-pin the engine exe in `campaign_frozen.json`; have preflight FAIL if the live exe sha
  changes without a recorded a6 PASS for that sha (or run a6 as an ~8s stage-0 sub-check every
  iteration). Optionally add a second-frame assertion `value_p1 == −value_p0` to the parity dump so the
  negation branch is under an automated gate (see M5).

#### M5 — The export-parity gate is architecturally blind to a shared-C++ feature bug AND the negation branch
- **Dimension:** orientation · **V: CONFIRMED (low→medium system-level)**
- **What:** Stage-5 parity feeds the **C++-extracted** features into both C++ and PyTorch and compares
  values; the dump evaluates from Player_One only (`NeuralNet.cpp:829`). So (1) the maxPlayer negation
  branch is dead code on the parity path, and (2) any feature-extraction bug present in the C++
  extractor flows identically into both sides and cancels.
- **Why unsound:** This is documented scope, but the campaign leans on this gate as the per-iteration
  green light while the two failure modes most relevant to value correctness (inversion; shared-C++
  feature skew — exactly the v2.2.1 + the new F-SKEW-1 class) are invisible to it, and the compensating
  gates (a6, three-way) are both manual (M4).
- **Fix:** Document parity scope at the stage-5 call site; automate+sha-pin a6 and three-way; add the
  `value_p1 == −value_p0` second-frame assertion.

#### M6 — Forced-Hotel curriculum trains 1/3 of data on a Hotel-saturated (100%) distribution
- **Dimension:** axis_premise · **V: NEEDS-NUANCE (medium)**
- **What:** The forced pool guarantees Hotel in 1 of 8 advanced slots in 100% of its games vs ~7.6%
  natural; the forced pool is both training curriculum and the d_rl measurement pool.
- **Why unsound:** Forcing a unit to surface a rare decision is legitimate, but a forced-pool gain can
  be overfit to the saturated co-occurrence rather than a deployable IG-timing skill. **Nuance:**
  transfer *is* measured — the **unforced general pool is the sole automated verdict input** — but only
  via aggregate win-rate; no panel isolates IG behaviour on natural boards.
- **Fix:** Add a natural-occurrence IG eval panel (Hotel allowed, not forced; record per-game IG
  click-rate) as the transfer check; treat forced-pool d_rl as a curriculum-fit metric.

#### M7 — Kill criterion references a per-iteration "improvement beyond CI" signal the harness never produces
- **Dimension:** eval_stats · **V: CONFIRMED (medium, doc-mismatch)**
- **What:** Kill rule = "≥3 consecutive iterations show no improvement beyond the CI". But per-iteration
  verdicts are only REJECT/REVIEW/INCOMPLETE (a harm screen); the only improvement number (origin) runs
  every 3–5 iters. The old GO improvement test was deleted.
- **Why unsound:** A pre-registered STOP condition that cannot be evaluated against any produced
  statistic at its stated cadence is not a real stop condition — it undermines the A9 / £400 discipline
  the campaign leans on.
- **Fix:** Rewrite against the checkpoint origin statistic and its real cadence (e.g. "N consecutive
  checkpoints with origin ci_lower not above 0.5 + clean triage").

#### M8 — Per-iteration REVIEW verdict is near-certain (~98% at parity) and drives no decision
- **Dimension:** eval_stats · **V: CONFIRMED (medium, statistics)**
- **What:** REVIEW = not-REJECT; P(REVIEW|parity)=97.7%, P(REVIEW|−3pp)=79%, P(REVIEW|−5pp)=51%.
  Promotion happens on REVIEW regardless, so the modal output drives nothing — at a cost of 576
  games/iter (384 general + 192 forced) at the 7s budget.
- **Why unsound:** Spending real eval compute on a screen whose output is ~constant. **Nuance:** it is
  not pure waste — it is a low-power-but-nonzero **catastrophe screen** (P(REJECT|−10pp) ≈ 0.98), a
  coherent safety floor for promote-unless-harm — but the informative middle range certifies nothing.
- **Fix:** Reallocate per-iteration eval games into a higher-cadence powered origin check, or justify
  explicitly what action a REVIEW (vs no eval) changes.

#### M9 — No trustworthy external/absolute strength signal exists
- **Dimension:** eval_stats · **V: CONFIRMED (medium)**
- **What:** All gating/checkpoint anchors (iter0, origin, narrow) are candidate-vs-a-net-from-the-same
  lineage (relative drift). The one external reference (steam vs the 2016 MasterBot) is cross-path
  (different harness each side; delta "effectively unbounded below ±20pp" until the deferred B4 check)
  AND uses a different draw estimand (wins/valid with draws-against-both) than the C++ anchors
  (win=(W+0.5D)/n) — demonstrated 0.45 vs 0.50 on 200g/20 draws.
- **Why unsound:** For a campaign whose purpose is "did RL make the net stronger", the absence of any
  sound external anchor means it can say "K beat K−1" but not whether the lineage is drifting up/down
  vs anything outside its own family. (Tempered: the primary claim is *relative* to the fixed v221
  origin, which the origin anchor does measure on one consistent path.)
- **Fix:** Add an absolute **same-path** strength anchor (lineage vs 2016 MasterBot through the C++
  tournament with the C++ draw convention); run the deferred 128-game cross-path calibration before
  treating steam as even a trend.

### LOW / OBSERVATION (grouped)

- **F-SKEW-1 — fragile-unit `is_blocking` freeze threshold — REPORTED THEN RETRACTED (2026-06-14, owner
  caught it).** The verifier claimed the SWF freezes fragiles only at *starting* HP while C++ freezes at
  current HP, and "proved" it with a constructed Forcefield (sh=2, dmg=1, chill=1, **blocking=true**).
  **This is a FALSE POSITIVE.** Ground truth re-checked against the AS3: the live freeze rule is
  `disruptDamage >= damageItCanTake + damage`, which equals **`current HP`** for both fragile and
  non-fragile (`Inst.as:224` `damageItCanTake = health − (fragile?0:damage)`; `State.as:1495`;
  `EndTurnObject.as:141` uses `disruptDamage >= inst.health` directly). At a **turn-start snapshot** a
  fragile unit's `damage=0` and `health` already carries the reduction, so the threshold is current HP —
  exactly what C++ `isFrozen() = currentChill() >= currentHealth()` computes. The C++ is **correct**.
  The verifier's mistake: it read `damageItCanTake + damage` as double-counting fragile damage (→ starting
  HP); and its "empirical proof" hand-set `blocking=true` on a unit that real play marks `blocking=false`
  the instant the chill lands (`State.as:1497`, `State.js:748`). The JS extractor reads the **stored**
  flag (`state_adapter.js:85`), so it returned the bogus `1`; C++ recomputed the correct `0`. On REAL
  turn-start data the stored flag is already consistent → JS and C++ **agree** (and the verifier's own
  209-sidecar sweep found **0** fragile+damaged+chilled instances, i.e. no live evidence ever existed).
  **No fix needed; do NOT change `isFrozen`.** Residual (low, non-manifesting): the C++ *recomputes*
  `is_blocking` rather than reading the serialized flag — verified to AGREE on real data (three-way gate
  green, 209 sidecars 0 mismatches), but it remains a re-derivation that must keep tracking the engine's
  freeze rule; the lesson is that adversarial verification can over-claim from a synthetic fixture, and
  any future blocking-feature check must use a real turn-start state, never a hand-built one.
- **Self-play exporter == inference verified clean otherwise** (feature_skew · positive). 209 real
  engine-native self-play sidecars / 11,264 instances → **0 mismatches** across all 10 instance feats
  with real frozen/blocking/lifespan/charge coverage; three-way gate 91/91; perspective round-trip
  13/13. The surface is clean apart from F-SKEW-1.
- **Three-way gate coverage is fixture-incidental** (feature_skew · low). 7 hand-picked fixtures; only
  structural assertion is "base units in_card_set=1". A future fixture edit could silently drop
  coverage of frozen/lifespan/etc. (exactly how all prior skews hid until fixtures were *expanded*).
  Fix: assert minimum per-feature coverage. Also: no **standing** exporter↔inference (no-JS) test over
  the per-iteration sidecars — stand one up at stage 5.
- **tau=0.7 tuned on the wrong population** (exploration · medium→ stat). The 41 tau-probe states have
  `numTurns` 15–24 (all **above** K=12), so they sit in the regime where tau-sampling **never runs**;
  real opening roots (turns<12) have median 3 children (vs probe 8–33). The "near-uniform → sharpen"
  justification doesn't apply to the population the knob governs. Fix: re-derive tau on real turn-<12
  states from the parity sidecars (impact bounded; tau=0.7 still gives ~24% opening diversity).
- **`ig_contrast_pairs` mispairs colour-swap games at Threads:8** (exploration · medium→low). The
  watch-stat pairs consecutive game ids, but ids are claimed in thread-execution order
  (`Tournament.cpp:336` fetch_add inside the async body), so ~12% of "pairs" are different card sets
  (reproduced: 14 match / 2 mismatch). It degrades a **redundant** diagnostic (per-record
  sampled_idx/argmax_idx already exist). Fix: pair by `card_set` (already in the record) or stamp a
  round id.
- **Self-play RNG is non-reproducible / not per-decision auditable at Threads:8** (exploration · low).
  One un-reseeded thread_local mt19937_64 per worker; fine for exploration, but a specific record's
  sampled IG count can't be re-derived from (state,net,seed). Fix (optional): derive a per-decision
  substream from (game_id, ply, seed).
- **Tie-break fix is self-play-only; eval/deploy keeps the over-click-biased first-wins tie-break**
  (axis_premise · low silent-skew). `MoveSampler::argmaxIndex` breaks ties uniformly (A1), but
  `getBestRootNode`/`mostVisitedChild` (eval/deploy) keeps strict first-index-wins, and AbilitySubset
  sorts longest-move-first → eval/deploy still prefers MORE IG clicks at genuine ties. So "A1 fixed
  over-clicking" is scoped to the generator only; the **deployed** AI keeps the bias. Impact bounded
